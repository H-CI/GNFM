GNFM: Generalizable NeRF with View-Aware Feature Modulation for Reflective Surgical Instrument Rendering toward Robot-assisted Surgery

Computational Visual Media Conference 2026

Lifei Xiao1, Zhenjie Zhao2*
1School of computer Science, Nanjing University of Information Science and Technology, 2Academy for Advanced Interdisciplinary Studies, College of Artificial Intelligence, Nankai University
Paper arXiv Video Code Dataset Demo

Overview

Accurate 3D reconstruction of small, highly reflective surgical instruments is essential for robust perception and safe operation in robot-assisted surgery. However, traditional multi-view reconstruction methods often fail to capture subtle geometry and view-dependent reflections, especially under specular surfaces and fine structures. Motivated by recent advances in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), we investigate their potential for reconstructing surgical instruments, where photorealistic rendering serves as an indicator of 3D surface fidelity. Despite their success, conventional NeRFs require dense per-scene training, lack cross-scene generalization, and struggle with specular highlights—limitations that are particularly problematic in surgical scenarios. To address these challenges, we propose GNFM, a Generalizable NeRF with View-Aware Feature Modulation, which extends the Generalizable NeRF Transformer (GNT) to enable robust and generalizable rendering of reflective surgical instruments.

Dataset Construction

We introduce RSID, a new dataset containing 8 categories of surgical instruments with 2,400 multi-view images, specifically designed to evaluate reconstruction and rendering under highly reflective conditions.

Key Contributions

Joint Multiresolution Hash Encoder (JMHE)

We introduce a Joint Multiresolution Hash Encoder that jointly encodes spatial positions and viewing directions. This design enhances the recovery of fine structures and surface details, which are critical for accurately modeling small and reflective surgical instruments.

View-Conditional Feature-wise Linear Modulation (V-FiLM)

We propose a View-Conditional Feature-wise Linear Modulation module that adaptively modulates neural features based on viewing direction, enabling improved rendering of specular highlights and other view-dependent effects.

Reflective Surgical Instrument Dataset (RSID)

We introduce RSID, a new dataset containing 8 categories of surgical instruments with 2,400 multi-view images, specifically designed to evaluate reconstruction and rendering under highly reflective conditions.

Method

GNFM builds upon the Generalizable NeRF Transformer (GNT) framework and augments it with view-aware and geometry-sensitive feature modeling. Specifically, JMHE performs joint spatial and directional encoding to strengthen fine-structure representation, while V-FiLM dynamically modulates intermediate features conditioned on viewing direction. Together, these components enable GNFM to better handle specular surfaces and view-dependent reflections, while preserving cross-scene generalization and maintaining computational efficiency comparable to existing methods.

Results

We evaluate GNFM on both synthetic and real-world reflective surgical instrument datasets. Experimental results show that GNFM consistently improves the recovery of fine structures and the rendering of specular surfaces. Quantitatively, GNFM achieves approximately 5% improvements over competitive baselines in metrics including PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, while maintaining comparable computational efficiency.
